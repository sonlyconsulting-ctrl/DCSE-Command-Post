## Verified source findings

- D20 identifies as v7.0 with a v6.9 parent.
- Its six-phase lifecycle ends at deployment and does not govern operation or retirement.
- Correction is treated as a return to Build rather than an accountable first-class phase.
- Product types and technical rules are disproportionately web and Wix focused.
- Several frontend, database, security, and test requirements are universalized when they should be product-specific.
- It requires successful physical registry writes without a verified schema adapter.
- It retains manual Level 0 promotion for every product transition.
- It lacks commercial readiness, operational ownership, cost controls, domain data integrity, and a mechanical completion contract.

## Comprehensive corrections applied

- Expanded the lifecycle to Intake, Discover, Plan, Build, Verify, Correct, Package, Promote, Deploy or Distribute, Operate, Reconcile, and Close or Retire.
- Added complete product declaration, acceptance, verification, package, operation, and completion contracts.
- Expanded product classes to applications, services, automation, data, games, media, local models, and combined products.
- Added current-state discovery and build-versus-buy-versus-reuse analysis.
- Added complete interface, data, worker, integration, environment, testing, resilience, and security requirements.
- Made correction and regression testing a mandatory executable loop.
- Added commercial readiness, support, licensing, privacy, fulfillment, analytics, cost, and margin considerations.
- Added product-specific data-integrity profiles, including sports and family products.
- Integrated corrected D05 promotion and D22 distribution and reconciliation behavior.
- Added degraded modes and prevented unverified Supabase schema assumptions.
- Added D20-001 through D20-020 mechanical acceptance tests.
