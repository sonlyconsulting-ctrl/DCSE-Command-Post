## 1. Assignment

Create the primary visual design for the Sonly Consulting main homepage.

This is not a generic consulting landing page, a software dashboard, or a gallery of disconnected products. It is the strategic front door to Sonly Consulting and a carefully curated working showroom of what the business can help people clarify, organize, design, and bring to life.

The design must communicate two truths at the same time:

1. Sonly Consulting provides structured consulting, business architecture, AI integration, digital-product development, and customer-facing systems.
2. Sonly Consulting can demonstrate that capability through real interactive experiences, tools, products, campaigns, and specialized digital builds.

Design one coherent primary direction. Do not produce multiple unrelated themes unless DCS later requests alternatives.

Do not write production code. Produce an editable, responsive, high-fidelity homepage design with clear component and interaction annotations.

---

## 2. Core Design Thesis

The homepage should feel like an experienced strategic consultant opened the door to a working studio.

It must combine:

- Executive credibility
- Human warmth
- Structural clarity
- Practical imagination
- Visible proof of capability
- Space for products and experiences to grow

The central principle is:

> Structure Precedes Scale.

The brand tagline is:

> Crafting Success on Your Own Terms.

The homepage should demonstrate the principle through its organization. Each section must have a defined purpose, a clear relationship to the next section, and an intentional conversion path.

The page should not merely tell visitors that Sonly Consulting is structured. The page itself must feel structured.

---

## 3. Intended User Outcome

Within the first screen, a visitor should understand:

- This is Sonly Consulting.
- Sonly Consulting helps turn ideas, experience, and business needs into organized offers, digital products, and practical customer-facing systems.
- The work is suited to people building on their own terms, including solopreneurs, freelancers, professionals in transition, adults age 45 and older, retirees exploring supplemental income, and small-business owners.
- A clear next step is available without pressure or hype.

After scrolling, the visitor should be able to:

- Recognize a problem Sonly Consulting can help address.
- Understand the principal service and product pathways.
- See selected examples of interactive and creative work.
- Distinguish Sonly Consulting from Smoove Spots.
- Enter the appropriate next step through Start Here, an assessment, a product experience, or a focused inquiry.

The page should follow this narrative:

1. **Recognition:** A serious idea deserves a structure capable of carrying it.
2. **Relevance:** Sonly Consulting helps people clarify what they provide, who it serves, and how the parts connect.
3. **Capability:** Strategy can become an offer, system, product, campaign, or interactive experience.
4. **Evidence:** Selected real builds show the range and quality of the work.
5. **Pathway:** Visitors can choose the kind of help or experience that fits the current need.
6. **Relationship:** Smoove Spots extends the enterprise into narrative and culture without confusing the SC identity.
7. **Action:** Start with a focused conversation or guided entry point.

Do not lead with technical terminology, database claims, internal governance, or product volume. Lead with what the visitor is trying to organize or create.

## 6. Required Page Structure

### Section 1: Navigation

Create a refined sticky header with restrained height and clear mobile behavior.

Recommended navigation:

- What SC Does
- Solutions
- Work and Experiences
- Product Studio
- Smoove Spots
- Start Here

Requirements:

- Use the approved Sonly Consulting logo asset if supplied.
- Do not create a replacement logo, fake logo, text-symbol logo, or decorative SVG.
- A subtle logo motion treatment may be annotated as optional, but the logo must not continuously rotate or distract from navigation.
- Make Start Here the clear primary navigation action.
- Provide a simple, accessible mobile menu.

### Section 2: Hero

Design a two-part hero that balances human meaning with visible structure.

The left side should carry the positioning and primary action. The right side should show a composed visual representation of how an idea becomes a structured outcome.

Working content hierarchy:

- Eyebrow: `Sonly Consulting`
- Primary headline: `Structure Precedes Scale.`
- Supporting idea: Sonly Consulting helps people organize business ideas, clarify offers, and develop practical digital products or customer-facing systems.
- Brand line: `Crafting Success on Your Own Terms.`
- Primary CTA: `Start Here`
- Secondary CTA: `Explore the Work`

The visual side must not look like a fake analytics dashboard. Create an elegant working-studio composition using real project previews, a restrained architecture map, or a sequence such as:

`Idea -> Clarity -> Structure -> Experience`

Use actual supplied screenshots or approved assets where possible. Do not use fake revenue charts, invented client counts, meaningless AI metrics, terminal code, or random database diagrams.

The hero should feel enterprise-capable but personally accessible. It should not resemble a large consulting corporation speaking to another corporation.

### Section 3: Audience Recognition

Create a compact transition section that helps the right visitor recognize a fit.

Avoid a long demographic list. Use concise, dignified language and visual groupings for:

- Solopreneurs and freelancers
- Professionals in transition
- Adults 45 and older building a next chapter
- Retirees exploring supplemental income
- Small-business owners organizing or modernizing an operation

Do not depict older adults as technologically confused, fragile, or behind. The visual posture should convey experience, capability, agency, and forward movement.

### Section 4: What Sonly Consulting Helps Build

Use three primary pillars.

#### Clarify the Direction

Organize the idea, audience, offer, positioning, and immediate priority.

#### Build the Structure

Connect workflows, content, AI capabilities, digital systems, and customer pathways.

#### Bring It to Life

Develop practical products, webpages, campaigns, assessments, and interactive experiences.

Each pillar should connect a human need to a concrete mechanism. Avoid generic feature lists and abstract buzzwords.

Do not overuse cards. This section may use a three-column composition on desktop, but it must become a clean vertical sequence on mobile.

### Section 5: Selected Work and Experiences

This is the working-showroom section.

Feature a curated set of no more than four experiences on the homepage. Prioritize quality, variety, and proof of range.

Two confirmed anchors are:

- Shoe Girl Platinum Birthday Experience
- Seven-Hand Spades Quiz

Reserve two additional positions for approved projects. Use neutral placeholders such as `Featured Experience` until names and assets are verified.

Each experience presentation should contain:

- Project or experience name
- One-sentence purpose
- Experience type
- Strong real screenshot, still, or supplied asset
- A concise capability demonstrated
- One action such as View Experience, Explore the Concept, or See How It Works

Do not display internal technical details as customer proof. Database schemas, idempotency rules, private admin tools, migration records, and internal governance are not homepage marketing content.

Preferred interaction:

- A composed editorial feature area with one lead experience and smaller supporting experiences
- Manual tabs, thumbnail selection, or controlled carousel navigation
- No forced auto-rotation
- No hidden essential content behind hover-only behavior
- Clear mobile stacking without cropped screenshots

### Section 6: Ways to Work With Sonly Consulting

Translate the service architecture into approachable entry paths. Do not present an overwhelming pricing table on the homepage.

Recommended paths:

#### Clarify

For a person who needs to organize the idea, offer, audience, or next move.

#### Strengthen

For an existing business or project with a defined bottleneck, scattered workflow, or incomplete system.

#### Build

For a person ready to develop a practical digital product, campaign, customer experience, or connected system.

These public labels are design and copy directions, not replacements for approved engagement names. The final service language must be reconciled with the current approved offer catalog before publication.

Include a restrained route to learn more or start the intake process.

### Section 7: Interactive Tools and Product Studio

Create one structured section that shows how SC thinking can become usable tools and products.

Use two clearly separated zones:

- **Try a Tool:** assessment, quiz, selector, or guided experience
- **Explore the Studio:** product families, journals, guides, educational experiences, and approved digital products

This section should feel useful, not like a storefront filled with unfinished inventory.

Display no more than three items at a time. Include a route to a future Products or Studio page where the full approved catalog can live.

Do not label candidate products as available. Use status-neutral presentation until release posture is verified.

### Section 8: Smoove Spots Doorway

Create a full-width narrative transition that introduces Smoove Spots as a related but separate destination.

The design should shift slightly in image rhythm, composition, and emotional temperature without introducing an unrelated brand palette or disrupting SC continuity.

The section should communicate:

- Smoove Spots is where story, culture, reflection, lifestyle, and memorable experiences receive more room.
- Visitors can move into SS intentionally.
- SC remains the strategic and commercial parent context for the current page.

Use a clear CTA such as `Enter Smoove Spots` or the currently approved destination label.

Do not call SS a sister venture unless DCS specifically approves that language.

### Section 9: The SC Method

Show a concise, understandable process:

1. Listen and clarify
2. Map the structure
3. Design the experience or system
4. Build and refine
5. Prepare the next move

Present this as a human working relationship, not a proprietary software pipeline.

The process should visually reinforce continuity and progression without using a long horizontal diagram that fails on mobile.

### Section 10: Final Invitation

End with a grounded invitation, not a high-pressure sales close.

Working hierarchy:

- Headline concept: `A strong idea deserves a structure that can carry it.`
- Supporting concept: Begin with the idea, challenge, or opportunity that needs to be organized.
- Primary CTA: `Start Here`
- Optional secondary CTA: `Explore Selected Work`

### Section 11: Footer

Create a professional footer with:

## 7. Visual Direction

### Overall Character

Use a premium, calm, modern visual system with warmth and breathing room.

The page should feel:

- Structured but not rigid
- Premium but not luxury theater
- Technical but not cold
- Soulful but not sentimental
- Contemporary but not trend-dependent
- Confident but not inflated

### Core Palette

Use SC navy and gold as the primary anchors.

Recommended starting tokens:

- Deep navy: `#0A192F`
- Structural navy: `#1B3A57`
- SC gold: `#D4AF37`
- Silver: `#C0C0C0`
- Warm off-white or restrained cream for light sections
- White and near-black only as needed for contrast

Gold should function as a controlled accent, not a full-page metallic effect.

Do not use orange-brown as the controlling SS or SC color direction. Do not introduce neon gradients, default browser blue, electric purple AI effects, or excessive emerald.

### Typography

Use a highly readable executive type system.

- Use Tahoma or a closely compatible humanist sans-serif for body copy and interface text unless the supplied active SC design system establishes a more current approved font.
- A restrained editorial serif may be used for limited display emphasis only if it strengthens the design and remains consistent with supplied brand assets.
- Avoid using condensed display fonts for paragraphs.
- Avoid oversized headlines that create awkward two-word line breaks.
- Maintain strong readability for adults across age groups.

### Layout

- Use a disciplined desktop grid with generous side margins.
- Alternate section density to create rhythm.
- Use full-width moments sparingly.
- Avoid deep card nesting.
- Keep card radii restrained, generally 8 pixels or less.
- Use visible alignment and consistent spacing tokens.
- Prevent the homepage from becoming a continuous wall of navy cards.
- Use light sections strategically to create relief and improve comprehension.

### Imagery

Prioritize:

- Real screenshots and stills from approved SC and SS experiences
- Human-centered business imagery with maturity, confidence, and individuality
- Adults actively creating, planning, presenting, building, or collaborating
- Images with natural light, deliberate composition, and room for text where needed

Avoid:

- Generic handshakes
- Stock conference rooms
- Robots, glowing brains, circuit-board faces, and floating AI symbols
- Fake dashboards
- Unverified customer imagery
- Images that stereotype age, retirement, race, or technical ability
- Crops that remove faces, heads, hands, or the essential subject

Use images at their natural aspect ratios where practical. Define separate desktop and mobile crop behavior when the same image cannot serve both formats cleanly.

### Motion

Use motion only to support orientation and polish.

Acceptable:

- Gentle reveal on scroll
- Subtle image transition
- Controlled hover state
- Manual experience selection
- Restrained logo or accent motion

Avoid:

- Continuous 3D logo rotation
- Auto-advancing essential content
- Perspective terminals
- Floating glass cards
- Parallax that reduces readability
- Motion that continues when reduced-motion preferences are enabled

## 9. Responsive Design Requirements

Produce and annotate desktop, tablet, and mobile designs.

Required review widths:

- Mobile portrait below 480 pixels
- Tablet and mobile landscape from 481 to 768 pixels
- Laptop from 769 to 1024 pixels
- Desktop above 1025 pixels

Requirements:

- Navigation must become a usable mobile menu.
- Hero content must reorder intentionally rather than simply shrink.
- Text must remain readable without extremely small type.
- Experience media must not crop unpredictably.
- Multi-column sections must stack in a meaningful order.
- Buttons must remain comfortably tappable.
- No horizontal scrolling.
- No nested scroll areas inside homepage sections.
- The mobile experience must preserve the same narrative order and conversion path.

For externally hosted interactive experiences embedded into Wix:

- Account for the current Wix host viewport constraint where applicable.
- Avoid nested scrollbars and clipped content.
- Design the homepage preview as a poster, screenshot, or controlled launch panel unless the full interactive module can be embedded responsively.
- Do not assume every product should run live inside the homepage.
- Do not reproduce the live SS desktop framing defect in which the embedded experience occupies only part of the available page width and leaves a large empty field beside it.
- Design the SC embed container to align with the intended content width, remain centered, and respond deliberately at every breakpoint.

## 10. Accessibility Target

Design the page to target WCAG 2.2 AA accessibility standards. Do not claim compliance without a formal audit.

Include:

- Strong text contrast
- Visible focus states
- Keyboard-operable navigation concepts
- Clear heading hierarchy
- Meaningful button labels
- Non-color status cues
- Reduced-motion behavior
- Readable body text and line length
- Image-alt-text requirements in the handoff notes
- No essential information communicated only through animation, hover, or imagery

## 11. Voice and Copy Controls

Public SC voice is approachable executive authority. It is framework-driven, clear, warm, direct, implementation-focused, and free from hype.

Apply:

`Voice x Audience x Intent x Persuasion Depth x Channel`

Move beyond features by connecting each capability to:

1. What it is
2. What it enables
3. What practical outcome matters
4. Why that outcome is personally relevant
5. How it supports the visitor's identity, next chapter, or larger story

Copy restrictions:

- Use third-person solo-brand posture.
- Do not use I, we, our, or us in public copy.
- Do not use em dashes or en dashes.
- Do not promise revenue, rapid success, guaranteed outcomes, or transformation.
- Do not invent metrics, testimonials, client history, partnerships, prices, or results.
- Do not expose internal doctrine, command structures, databases, agent routing, security details, or governance terminology.
- Do not use generic AI hype.
- Keep paragraphs compact and readable.
- Use `GET YOUR THINK ON!™` only with the approved mark and only where contextually appropriate.

The current approved tagline is `Crafting Success on Your Own Terms.` Treat older source wording such as `Crafting Success on Determined Terms` as superseded unless DCS expressly restores it.
