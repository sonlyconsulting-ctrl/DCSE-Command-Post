## 7. Tool-Stack Decision

This project supports a hybrid production architecture:

- Paid tools for high-value capabilities such as dependable voice, presenter generation, premium enhancement, rapid editing, commercial-use clarity, and production throughput.
- Open-source tools for local rendering, repeatable processing, transcription, audio cleanup, encoding, resizing, captioning, quality control, asset tracking, and batch automation.
- Model-routing logic that sends each stage to the lowest-cost tool that meets the defined quality threshold.
- Human or governed agent approval at concept lock, asset selection, final cut, and publication.

CapCut and HeyGen should be evaluated as paid candidates, not automatically adopted. Selection must consider commercial rights, export restrictions, watermark rules, voice licensing, API or automation support, batch limits, cost per approved minute, and exit portability.

## 8. Bulk Video Production Module

Module name: `DCSE Bulk Video Production Module`  
Working identifier: `DCSE-BVPM`  
Owning lane: `SC Production`  
Governance posture: `Structure Precedes Scale`

### 8.1 Objective

Convert approved campaign briefs and asset sets into governed batches of short-form videos, supporting assets, landing-page packages, and contribution receipts without losing lineage, rights data, cost visibility, or quality control.

### 8.2 Required Inputs

- Campaign ID and lane.
- Campaign objective.
- Audience.
- Destination and CTA.
- Brand and voice profile.
- Source text, images, audio, video, and URLs.
- Output formats, aspect ratios, durations, languages, and channel targets.
- Rights and consent status for every source asset.
- Budget ceiling.
- Deadline and priority.
- Required human approval gates.

### 8.3 Production Pipeline

1. Intake and validation.
2. Campaign brief lock.
3. Story, script, and shot-list generation.
4. Source-asset generation or ingestion.
5. Asset normalization and rights check.
6. Video generation by one or more routed models.
7. Battle-of-model comparison when the value justifies the cost.
8. Voice, music, sound, captions, and accessibility pass.
9. Edit, assembly, branding, and channel variants.
10. Automated technical quality control.
11. Human or governed-agent creative review.
12. Final render and derivative exports.
13. Landing-page, SEO, AEO, GEO, and social package generation.
14. Publication or handoff.
15. Contribution receipts, cost record, hashes, and promotion record.

### 8.4 Batch Controls

- Campaign-level and item-level job IDs.
- Idempotent retries.
- Versioned prompts and settings.
- Asset hashes and parent-child lineage.
- Per-stage status and error codes.
- Provider fallback rules.
- Concurrency and rate-limit controls.
- Budget stop-gates.
- Rights and privacy stop-gates.
- Manual hold, resume, reject, and promote controls.
- Variant matrix by aspect ratio, duration, language, CTA, and channel.

### 8.5 Core Output Variants

- 16:9 landscape.
- 9:16 vertical.
- 1:1 square.
- 4:5 social feed.
- Captioned and non-captioned masters.
- Voice and music alternatives.
- Platform-safe short versions.
- Thumbnail and social-share images.
- Transcript and caption files.
- Landing-page copy and metadata.
- Structured-data template.
- Participant receipts and production manifest.

### 8.6 Quality Gates

- Story and message match the approved brief.
- Characters, brand elements, dates, and critical objects remain consistent.
- Voice pronunciation and timing are approved.
- Captions match spoken audio.
- No clipped text, missing limbs, unintended artifacts, broken motion, or unsafe frames.
- Audio loudness, silence, peaks, and channel balance meet the target profile.
- Codec, resolution, frame rate, duration, and file size meet destination requirements.
- CTA and destination are correct.
- Rights, consent, and commercial-use status are recorded.
- Final output receives an explicit promotion decision.

### 8.7 Dashboard Requirements

- Batch queue and job state.
- Campaign brief and asset manifest.
- Storyboard and shot preview.
- Side-by-side model battle review.
- Cost by provider, model, campaign, and approved minute.
- Render time, active time, wait time, and total time-to-market.
- Revision and rejection reasons.
- Rights and consent status.
- Voice and music license status.
- Final-output preview and publication destinations.
- Contribution receipts by participant.
- Audit export for GitHub, Supabase, and Tribunal or governed receipt storage as authorized by lane.

### 8.8 Initial MVP

The first MVP should support:

- One campaign brief producing 5 to 25 short videos.
- Reusable character and brand packs.
- Image-to-video and text-to-video routing.
- Voiceover, background audio, captioning, and logo/end-card assembly.
- 16:9, 9:16, and 1:1 exports.
- Local FFmpeg-based normalization and quality checks.
- One paid provider and one open-source/local fallback per critical stage where feasible.
- Model battle on selected items rather than every item.
- Wix-ready landing-page copy and metadata.
- Automatic participant receipts, asset manifests, hashes, costs, and timing.
