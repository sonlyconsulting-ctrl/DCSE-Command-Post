WEEK 1: EVIDENCE & EXPERIMENTATION

Day 1 | Testing Arguments

Theme Overview: Strong arguments need testing. Experimenting with evidence validates or refines your reasoning.

Mini-Framework: Tiny Experiment: Design a small test for an argument (hypothesis, action, outcome).

🧠 Thinker's Tip: Treat every argument as a hypothesis to test, not a truth to defend.

Visual Aid: Outline a 'Tiny Experiment' you could run in an everyday context, whether it's improving a personal habit, testing a new creative technique, or understanding a social dynamic. Clearly state your Hypothesis, the Action you would take, and the Outcome you would measure. Reflect on what you hope to learn from this experimental cycle. Response Area: Describe your visual aid (up to 100 words)

💡 Prompt 1: Write an argument you want to test in real life.

Response Area: Up to 25 words

✏️ Prompt 2: Design a Tiny Experiment: State your hypothesis, action, and expected outcome. What evidence will you collect?

Response Area: Between 50–75 words

🎯 Prompt 3: How might confirmation bias affect your experiment’s design or interpretation?

Response Area: Between 50–100 words

🔄 Bonus: Reflect on: “Failure is simply the opportunity to begin again.” —Henry Ford. How does this relate to testing arguments?

Response Area: 10-word summary + 100-word explanation

Dig Deeper: Explain testing arguments to a colleague using a workplace example.

Response Area: Between 50–100 words

Day 2 | Evidence in Action

Theme Overview: Evidence drives effective experiments. Evaluating sources ensures your actions are grounded in reliable data.

Mini-Framework: Source Experiment: Test a source’s credibility by acting on its advice and observing results.

🧠 Thinker's Tip: Act on evidence, but verify its source first.

Visual Aid: Choose a piece of information (e.g., from an article, a friend's advice, a social media post) that influenced a recent decision. Describe how you would evaluate its source using a checklist approach: considering the source's 'Expertise', potential 'Bias', and the 'Verifiability' of the information. Reflect on how this critical evaluation impacts your trust and subsequent actions. Response Area: Describe your visual aid (up to 100 words)

💡 Prompt 1: Write about a source (e.g., article, advice) shaping a recent decision.

Response Area: Up to 25 words

✏️ Prompt 2: Design a Source Experiment: Act on the source’s advice. How will you measure results? Assess expertise, bias, and verifiability.

Response Area: Between 50–75 words

🎯 Prompt 3: How might you adjust your action if the source proves unreliable?

Response Area: Between 50–100 words

🔄 Bonus: Reflect on: “Knowledge is power.” —Francis Bacon. How does source reliability impact action?

Response Area: 10-word summary + 100-word explanation

Dig Deeper: Compare your source evaluation today with a year ago.

Response Area: Between 50–100 words

Day 3 | Correlation vs Causation

Theme Overview: Acting on correlations without testing causation can lead to flawed outcomes. Experiments clarify true causes.

Mini-Framework: Causal Experiment: Test if one event causes another (action, measure, analyze).

🧠 Thinker's Tip: Correlation isn’t causation—test before acting.

Visual Aid: Describe a pattern you've observed in your daily life or work where you might have assumed one thing caused another (e.g., 'Every time I do X, Y happens'). Outline the two 'Correlated Trends' and then explain how you would design a simple 'Causal Experiment' to test if one truly causes the other. Reflect on the importance of testing assumptions before acting. Response Area: Describe your visual aid (up to 100 words)

💡 Prompt 1: Write a pattern where you assumed one thing caused another.

Response Area: Up to 25 words

✏️ Prompt 2: Design a Causal Experiment: Describe your action, measurement, and analysis to test causation.

Response Area: Between 50–75 words

🎯 Prompt 3: How might overlooking alternative causes skew your experiment?

Response Area: Between 50–100 words

🔄 Bonus: Propose a follow-up experiment to confirm your findings.

Response Area: Experiment + 100-word explanation

Dig Deeper: Ask someone’s opinion on your pattern and why.

Response Area: Between 50–75 words

Day 4 | Overcoming Decision Paralysis

Theme Overview: Decision paralysis stalls action. Small experiments break the cycle by testing options quickly.

Mini-Framework: Triple Check (Head, Heart, Hand): Analyze (logic), feel (intuition), act (experiment).

🧠 Thinker's Tip: Start small to build momentum; indecision is worse than a wrong move.

Visual Aid: Recall a decision you're currently stuck on. Describe how you would apply the 'Triple Check' framework: what does your 'Head' (logic/analysis) tell you, what does your 'Heart' (intuition/emotion) feel, and what small 'Hand' (action/experiment) could you take to move forward? Reflect on how aligning these three aspects helps overcome indecision. Response Area: Describe your visual aid (up to 100 words)

💡 Prompt 1: Write about a decision you’re stuck on.

Response Area: Up to 25 words

✏️ Prompt 2: Using Triple Check, analyze logically, check your intuition, and propose a small action to test.

Response Area: Between 50–75 words

🎯 Prompt 3: What fears or biases might be stalling your action?

Response Area: Between 50–100 words

🔄 Bonus: Reflect on: “Action is the antidote to despair.” —Joan Baez. How does this relate to decision paralysis?

Response Area: 10-word summary + 100-word explanation

Dig Deeper: Explain decision paralysis to a friend using a relatable example.

Response Area: Between 50–100 words

Day 5 | Learning from Failure

Theme Overview: Failures are data points for growth. Experiments turn setbacks into insights.

Mini-Framework: Failure Analysis: Describe failure, identify causes, propose next steps.

🧠 Thinker's Tip: Every failure teaches something—don’t waste the lesson.

Visual Aid: Describe a recent setback or 'failure' in a personal or professional endeavor. Outline the 'Failure Analysis' cycle: what was the 'Action', what was the 'Failure', what 'Insight' did you gain, and what 'Next Action' will you take based on that learning? Reflect on how viewing failures as data points fuels growth. Response Area: Describe your visual aid (up to 100 words)

💡 Prompt 1: Write about a recent failure or setback.

Response Area: Up to 25 words

✏️ Prompt 2: Using Failure Analysis, describe the failure, its causes, and one actionable insight.

Response Area: Between 50–75 words

🎯 Prompt 3: How can you design a new experiment based on this insight?

Response Area: Between 50–100 words

🔄 Bonus: Reflect on: “I have not failed. I’ve just found 10,000 ways that won’t work.” —Thomas Edison. How does this relate to failure?

Response Area: 10-word summary + 100-word explanation

Dig Deeper: Share your failure insight with a colleague to spark discussion.

Response Area: Between 50–100 words

Day 7 | Lateral Action

Theme Overview: Lateral thinking tests unconventional approaches through action, breaking from routine solutions.

Mini-Framework: IF-THEN Experiment: Test an unconventional “if” to a “then” outcome.

🧠 Thinker's Tip: Ask: “What if the opposite were true?” to spark new actions.

Visual Aid: Think of a problem you've approached conventionally. Describe an 'unconventional' or 'lateral' action you could take that challenges the norm. Outline the 'IF-THEN Experiment' for this: 'IF' you take this unconventional action, 'THEN' what unexpected outcome might occur? Reflect on how breaking from routine can lead to breakthroughs. Response Area: Describe your visual aid (up to 100 words)

Day 8 | Adaptive Mental Models

Theme Overview: Mental models guide action, but they must adapt to new evidence. Experimenting refines them.

Mini-Framework: Model Experiment: Act on a mental model, observe results, update.

Outline a 'Model Experiment': what 'Action' would you take based on your current model, what 'Results' would you observe, and how would you 'Update' your model if reality didn't match? Reflect on the importance of adapting your internal maps to new evidence. Response Area: Describe your visual aid (up to 100 words)
